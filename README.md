# SEA-HAF

Southeast Asia Hybrid Air-quality Forecast: a hybrid physics–statistics–machine-learning PM2.5 forecast for mainland Southeast Asia (formerly "Hotspot Smoke Watch"; the repository name and site address are unchanged).

Hourly PM2.5 warning page for Northern Thailand and mainland Southeast Asia.

- **Total PM2.5 forecast**: CAMS global forecast (via Open-Meteo), corrected every hour against the measurements (Thailand: hourly values read directly from Air4Thai, the Pollution Control Department, plus the Bangkok (BMA) feed of 24-h means; elsewhere OpenAQ reference monitors and low-cost sensors) with a per-station Kalman filter on log-ratio bias (`CFG.kf`; also a filter on CAMS + the fire-assimilation increment and a slow one), then, at the hourly stations, the self-learning correction (`scripts/ml.mjs`, leads ≤ 24 h; not at the 24-h-mean stations, i.e. the BMA feed and OpenAQ's copies of Air4Thai). The map is corrected by regression kriging (`scripts/rk.mjs`, `CFG.rk`) of the 24-h means at the reference monitors and the low-cost sensors that pass their neighbour check: ln(obs+1) regressed on ln(CAMS+1) (CAMS + the fire-assimilation increment in the version the pages show), elevation (DEM, 0.05°), ln(BLH) and 100-m wind speed, residuals kriged on a 0.025° grid (`data/rk-bias*.json`; at least 15 inputs, else skipped); the page applies it to the hourly CAMS field with a fade over lead (e-folding 48 h), and on top of it the ML correction of the monitors kriged to 0.1° every 3 h to 24 h (`CFG.mlField`, `data/ml-field.json`). Hourly Air4Thai values first pass a spike check (`scripts/obsqc.mjs`): a value above 300 µg/m³ and more than 5 times the largest of the 3 hours before it is an instrument fault and is dropped, at intake and when the logs are read back for the daily checks (25 Nov 2026: 904 and 998 µg/m³ at a4t:19t between 54 and 37 had pushed that station's Kalman bias to about ×30). Since 27 Nov 2026 the same rule covers the stations that give only their latest value (OpenAQ sensors, the Bangkok feed), against their own last 3 hours; and when a dropped spike's hour had already been taken in by a station's Kalman filters, those filters are started again once (`resetAfterSpikes`), in the CAMS and the SILAM run alike. A station's newest reading that the check holds back is still published (`stations[].held` in `latest.json`): the page shows it at once on the map and in the station list, marked as unconfirmed, while no correction uses it. Since 28 Nov 2026 two more checks hold readings back the same way (decisions logged in `qc-YYYYMMDD.csv` on the `history` branch): a neighbour spike test on the Air4Thai hourly stations (a jump of 40 µg/m³ or more to at least 3 times the median of the 3 hours before, which the stations within 50 km do not share, is held; it is dropped if the station falls back within the next hours and kept if the level lasts or the neighbours join: 17 events, 20 hours on the Oct–Nov 2026 logs, among them Loei's 104 µg/m³ of 27 Nov and a4t:50t's 153 and 221 of 20 Nov, which Air4Thai itself later revised to 23 and 21), and a stuck-at-zero test on every station with hourly values (12 hours at or below 0.5 µg/m³ while the stations within 100 km have a median of 10 or more, or 48 hours for a sensor without neighbours; it stays out until it reports above 0.5 again, and its zeros are left out of the 24-h means).
- **Fire smoke**: VIIRS hotspots from NASA FIRMS (S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21 NRT, last 2 days) → FRP-based PM2.5 emission (Wooster et al. 2005; emission factor by fuel type from MODIS IGBP land cover, FINN v2.5 (Wiedinmyer et al. 2023), which takes most factors from Akagi et al. 2011; diurnal fire cycle after Zhang et al. 2020) → plume rise (Sofiev et al. 2012; the part above the boundary layer is dropped) → Gaussian puff dispersion with ECMWF IFS winds from Open-Meteo (Briggs 1973 σ with Turner 1970 stability classes, Heffter 1965 long-range σy) and below-cloud wet removal (HYSPLIT default scavenging, Λ = 8×10⁻⁵·P^0.79 s⁻¹; observed rain in the past hours). The model code between `/*MODEL-START*/` and `/*MODEL-END*/` in `index.html` runs in the page and, loaded by `scripts/model.mjs`, in the pipeline's fire-emission assimilation (`scripts/assim.mjs`): each hour the emission of each fire group (0.5° × local solar day) is rescaled from the last ~48 h of reference-monitor measurements (Bayesian inversion, prior factor-of-two uncertainty), and the resulting increment is added to CAMS. CAMS run only: SILAM carries its own fire emissions.
- **Warnings**: towns and stations banded on Thailand's PCD PM2.5 scale (1 June 2023). One rule everywhere: a forecast 24-h mean above 37.5 µg/m³ or an hour above 75 µg/m³; a district is warned when a station in it has measured a 24-h mean above 37.5, when its own mean forecast passes the rule, or when a town or station in it does.
- **Verification**: forecasts are logged and scored against later observations by lead time (`data/verify.json`).
- **Multi-year station background** (`scripts/climatology.mjs`, workflow `climatology.yml`, built once by hand): the last 3 years of PM2.5 of every reference monitor from the OpenAQ archive on S3 (for the Air4Thai stations, OpenAQ's copies, which hold 24-h running means: the month level is right, the hour of day is flattened; the Air4Thai history service keeps only 2–3 months and fills in only stations without a copy) summarised by month and hour (mean and 90th percentile) in `data/static/pm-clim.json`. The correction v2 reads them as two inputs (`lnClimMH`, `lnP90MH`), so it starts the burning season knowing what each station usually measures then instead of nothing; its experts still decide, from the daily check, how much to use them.
- **Compared with IQAir** (`scripts/iqair.mjs`, page layer *AQI compared with IQAir (now)*): IQAir's real-time US AQI for 17 Thai cities and 10 in Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, Vietnam and Malaysia (AirVisual API, free Community plan: city AQI only, no forecast; 9 cities per hourly run in groups of 5 a minute apart, run beside the other steps, so each city about every 3 h, about 6,700 calls a month; key in the `IQAIR_API_KEY` secret, never logged). IQAir is the reference: the labels give the share of hours over 7 days in which our 1-h forecast at the nearest forecast point (within 15 km) fell in the same Thai concentration band as IQAir (`accuracy.json` `iqair`, `scoreIqair` in `scripts/accuracy.mjs`), and the tooltip the values now beside our map. Agreement with IQAir, not accuracy against measurements. Data credited to IQAir.
- **Hindcast expert** (`scripts/hindcast.mjs`, trained once by `scripts/hindcast_train.mjs` into `data/static/hc-model.json`): gradient-boosted trees learned from three burning seasons (18 Dec – 30 Apr of 2023/24–2025/26) of archived CAMS forecasts paired with Air4Thai 24-h means (branch `hindcast`, `data/hindcast/`; contains modified Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service information). In a leave-one-season-out test it cut the RMSE of the CAMS 24-h mean 2–3 days ahead from 17–25 to 14–15 µg/m³ and caught 61–68 % of days >= 37.5 instead of 25–52 %, with fewer false alarms than persistence. It joins the self-learning correction as one more expert (CAMS run only, active 18 Dec – 30 Apr), fixed rather than retrained; the daily scoring gives it weight only while it helps.
- **Pull of long-lead forecasts toward the climatology (prepared, off)**: forecast skill falls with lead, so days 3–5 may do better pulled toward each station's usual value for the month and hour, ln(B+1) = (1−w)·ln(F+1) + w·ln(C+1). The runs log the published forecast and the climatology every 3 h at leads 24–120 h (`climfc-*.csv`, CAMS and SILAM separately); `scripts/fit_clim_blend.mjs` fits w by lead and keeps it only where it lowers the RMSE and catches at least as many hours ≥ 37.5 µg/m³ with no larger false-alarm share. `CFG.climBlend` stays zero until then. The code that applies it is in place: `scripts/climblend.mjs` applies any non-zero weight to the published series of the hourly reference monitors (the map and the logs keep the forecast before the blend); with all weights zero nothing changes.
- **Second forecast model, SILAM, through the same chain** (`scripts/silam.py`, `scripts/silam.mjs`, `scripts/silam_chain.mjs`): the FMI SILAM global air-quality forecast (0.2°, hourly, about 5 days per daily run; open data on AWS S3, CC BY 4.0). Its NetCDF-4 files are read by one Python step (netCDF4, installed only when a new run is to be read); the crop of our box is kept on the `history` branch (`silam-YYYYMMDD.json`). Then `silam_chain.mjs` runs `update.mjs` a second time with `HSW_MODEL=silam`: the same program with SILAM in the place of CAMS, so SILAM gets the steps CAMS gets (observation checks, Kalman filters, station forecasts, self-learning corrections v1/v2 (without the hindcast expert), nowcast, 0.025° map correction, forecast logs), except the fire-emission assimilation (SILAM has its own fire emissions). The two runs are kept apart: SILAM's filter and learning state rides in `state.json` as `silamState`, its logs go to `data/history/silam/` and `data/mltrain/silam/`, its page files to `data/silam/`; the measurements are read back from the replies the CAMS run kept (`HSW_HTTP_CACHE`), and the model-independent steps (radar, observed rain, temperature, weather learning) are left to the CAMS run. On the admin page (`/dev.html`; the public pages show CAMS only), *Total PM2.5 forecast from SILAM (~5 days)* loads `data/silam/latest.json` in the place of `data/latest.json`, so the whole page (map, warnings, summary) shows the SILAM-based forecast; the accuracy card stays CAMS until SILAM is scored.
- **Ask SEA-HAF (chat button, every page)**: a chat button at the bottom right (the AQI-ring "S" icon, `assets/sea-haf-bot-*.svg`) answers questions in spoken, conversational Thai (or casual English), only from what the page has loaded (no outside service or AI model). It covers:
  - PM2.5 now / today / tomorrow / the day after, at the viewer's location or any Thai province (the same values as the map; a province = the mean of its 0.1° points), with the Thai AQI;
  - health advice by the PCD band of the 24-h mean;
  - the worst and cleanest provinces, hotspots near a place, and weather;
  - the 7-day accuracy, and short notes on what PM2.5 / AQI are and where the data come from.
  Questions are matched by keywords, a province name (Thai or English, with a few common short names), a day word and a part of the day (เช้า / สาย / กลางวัน / บ่าย / เย็น / ค่ำ–ดึก: the answer covers those hours only); quick-question chips are offered. Fog is not forecast: asked about fog ("หมอก", not "หมอกควัน" = smoke haze, which is PM2.5), the bot judges it from the ECMWF humidity and 10-m wind of those hours (morning 04–10 h by default) and says so.
- **Main site on Cloudflare Pages** (`cloudflare/pages/`, https://sea-haf.pages.dev; GitHub Pages, https://smalltee2.github.io/hotspot-smoke-watch/, is the backup with the same pages): the hourly run also uploads the same `_site` with wrangler (direct upload from GitHub Actions; Cloudflare does not build the repository), plus the Pages Functions `functions/api/feedback.js` / `export.js`. The pages carry `<link rel="canonical">` to pages.dev, and the About tab links both sites.
  - The Cloudflare step is skipped until the `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` / `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` secrets are set, and a Cloudflare failure never stops the GitHub deploy.
  - `.github/workflows/cloudflare-setup.yml` (run once by hand) creates the Pages project, the D1 database `sea-haf-db` (`schema.sql`), the KV namespace `sea-haf-kv` and the Pages secret `EXPORT_TOKEN`, then writes the D1 / KV ids into `cloudflare/pages/wrangler.toml`.
  - All page paths are relative, so the site works at the root of pages.dev and under `/hotspot-smoke-watch/` on GitHub.
- **Ask SEA-HAF learns** (`scripts/chatml.mjs`, `chat/`): which topic a question is about is decided by a small classifier.
  - **Model:** character 2–4-grams hashed into 8,192 buckets and multinomial logistic regression, int8 weights (`chat/model.json`, about 140 KB, loaded when the chat opens). It is trained by `scripts/chat_train.mjs` from template questions (`scripts/chat_data.mjs`) plus the questions viewers labelled (`chat/learned.json`).
  - **Training data** (`chat/data/`, see its README): the templates; about 1,400 paraphrases written for the 13 topics (casual, polite and formal Thai, Northern Thai and Isan, Thai mixed with English, English; 15 % kept apart as the development set); 2,800 rows of Amazon MASSIVE 1.1 in Thai and English (weather → wx, greetings → hi, other assistant requests → other; CC BY 4.0, FitzGerald et al. 2022); each varied by `scripts/chat_augment.mjs` (misspellings, dropped tone marks, spoken forms, particles, short place names, spacing), topics balanced. On the test questions the linear model went from 92 % to 96 %.
  - **Neural network:** `scripts/chat_train.mjs` also trains a small network (one hidden layer of 32 or 48 ReLU units on the same features) and keeps whichever does better on the development set; with this data the linear model still wins (development 82 % vs 75 %), so it stays. The page reads either kind.
  - **Accuracy:** on the separate test questions (`chat/test.json`, never trained on) it gets 96 %, against 66 % for the keyword rules.
  - **Questions it was unsure of** (top probability under 0.6) are sent too (kind unsure), and in the **collecting mode** (a link with `?chatlab=1`, for a class trying the bot; a notice says so) every typed question (kind collect; 600 a day per sender). The admin labels them on the dev page ("Chat questions to label", method tab; key = the EXPORT_TOKEN value, kept in the browser tab only; `functions/api/label.js`, table `chat_label`). The weekly job puts a fixed third of the admin's labels into `chat/test_real.json` (real test questions, never trained on) and the rest into the training rows.
  - **Local AI suggestions** (`scripts/chat_llm_label.mjs`, run by the admin on their own computer with Ollama or LM Studio; stand-alone, Node 18+): a local LLM (default `qwen2.5:7b`) reads the labelling queue and suggests a topic for each question (`functions/api/suggest.js`, table `chat_suggest`). The dev page shows the suggestion as a ✨ button; only the admin's click makes it a label, so the real test questions stay checked by a person. The questions go only to the LLM on that computer and back to the site; the key is typed or read from the environment, never saved.
  - **Questions written by the local AI** (`node chat_llm_label.mjs --generate 30`): the model writes about 30 questions per topic in varied styles (casual, polite, formal, Northern Thai, Isan, Thai-English, typos, English) and keeps only those it classifies back to the same topic; they go to `/api/generated` (table `chat_gen`), are exported as kind llmgen and become training rows (counted once, newest 4,000), never test questions; a question people labelled keeps their label.
  - **Daily on the Mac** (`--save-key` once: the key goes into the login Keychain; `--install-daily 07:41`: a launchd job runs `--daily` every day, i.e. topic suggestions for the queue, then 10 new questions a topic; log `~/Library/Logs/sea-haf-chat-llm.log`; `--uninstall-daily` removes it). The Mac and Ollama must be on; a day missed while asleep runs on waking. Each daily run first reads the site's `chat/model.json` and shows a macOS notification when the weekly training switched the model's kind (linear → small neural network, or back); `--test-notify` shows a test one. The installed copy updates itself each day from this repository's `main` branch (only a file that looks like the script and passes `node --check`; `--no-update` turns it off). For the topic `other` the model gets its own request (everyday questions without places or times) and questions that still name dust, smoke, fire, fog or weather are dropped.
  - **How the page uses it:** below a probability of 0.5 the rules decide; with neither, the bot asks "which do you mean?". Every answer offers "not what I asked?".
  - **Learning from viewers:** the topic a viewer picks is sent with the question (nothing else; no phone numbers, e-mails or links; viewers can switch it off) to the Cloudflare Pages site's function `/api/feedback` (D1 database; `cloudflare/pages/`; its address goes in `chat/config.json`). The weekly workflow `.github/workflows/chat-train.yml` reads `/api/export` with the `CHAT_EXPORT_TOKEN` secret (`scripts/chat_learn.mjs`: majority label of at least 2/3, at most 200 new questions a run), retrains, and keeps the old model when the test accuracy would drop by more than 1 point.
  - Places, days and parts of the day are still read by rules, and every answer is still computed from the data.

Every image names the places: a chosen province gets its district (bold) and sub-district names and borders, any other area its province names; names never overlap. - **One accuracy everywhere (since 26 Nov 2026, owner's request)**: the map badges, the card in the *Warnings* tab, *My location*, the line under the map item and the chat all show the same number, **accuracy = 100 − NRMSE** (NRMSE = RMSE ÷ mean measured value × 100) of the daily mean PM2.5 (00–24 h Thai time) forecast 24 h ahead (and 48 h in the card), over the last 30 days at the reference monitors (`accuracy.json` `m1` / `m2`); the *About* tab explains it with an example. Low-cost sensors are scored apart the same way; IQAir with the same measure on its hourly values over 7 days. The older measures below (colour-band share, hourly horizons, R², MAPE) are still computed in `accuracy.json` and the run log for the checks, not shown.
- **How right the forecast has been, on the public page** (`scripts/accuracy.mjs` → `data/accuracy.json`, rebuilt every run): the published hourly PM2.5 forecast against the hourly reference monitors over the last 7 days, per monitor, in three horizons (next hours: issued 1, 3, 6 h before; next day: 12, 24 h before; two days ahead: 48 h before, shown as one line under the table; pooling all leads would flatter the score, since the 1-h forecast is close to the latest measurement). The page shows Thailand only (outside it there are too few hourly reference monitors; one in Nov 2026) and sums the monitors for *here* (the nearest monitor within 25 km, else the Thai region), a Thai region (the TMD regions of the region menu) and Thailand, and shows the share of hours in the right Thai PCD colour band, the typical error (MAE), the tendency (bias) and, on the admin page only, how many hours ≥ 37.5 µg/m³ were warned in time and how many warnings did not happen. The same colour-band score is also given on the daily mean (24-h, the Thai AQI basis): the published 24-h mean forecast 24 h and 48 h before against the measured 24-h mean (≥ 18 measured hours), only at those two leads, since at shorter ones the 24 hours are partly measured already (one line under the table, and in the card's heading). Shown in the *Warnings* tab (*How right has the forecast been?*), in the *My location* card, and as an optional map layer of badges at the monitors (*Show on map*): since 26 Nov 2026 each badge (since 26 Nov 2026, owner's choice, after the usual normalised error of air-quality model evaluation) shows one number, the accuracy = 100 − NRMSE (NRMSE = RMSE as a % of the mean measured value; robust at low concentrations, where MAPE is not), with a bar of the same length; the hover box adds the NRMSE, the typical error in µg/m³ and the tendency (no hourly scores there). The colour-band share stays in the card of the Warnings tab. They are scored on the daily mean (calendar day 00–24 h Thai time, the Thai AQI basis, measured from >= 18 hours) forecast 24 h ahead over the **last 30 days** (`accuracy.json` `m1`, 48 h ahead `m2`; read from the runs issued at 17 UTC, so about 30 independent days per monitor: 7 days of running means gave too few for a monitor's R²; >= 15 days for a badge; the hover box also gives 48 h ahead and the 7-day hourly scores; the next-day hourly score is used where no daily score exists yet). On the owner's live-evaluation record (2 Oct – 25 Nov 2026, 76 monitors with >= 20 days in the last 30; the logs of that period are no longer all on the `history` branch, which keeps 35 days, while the maintainer's computer keeps the full archive, so it cannot be rerun from the repository alone): R² 0.67 pooled, 0.41 per-monitor median, MAPE 19 %, against raw CAMS 0.35 / 0.20 / 41 %, **R²** (square of Pearson's r of the paired forecasts and measurements: how well the forecast follows the ups and downs) and **MAPE** (mean |forecast − measured| / measured, in %, over the values measured ≥ 10 µg/m³: below that a 1-µg/m³ miss is tens of per cent) below; the hover box adds the error in µg/m³ and the tendency, and the line under the map item gives both for all the monitors of the area shown together (pooled hours, so R² there also counts the differences between monitors). `accuracy.json` carries the sums for them (columns 8–13, `cols`). Low-cost sensors are left out of these numbers and scored apart (`accuracy.json` `lowcost`): the OpenAQ low-cost sensors that pass the pipeline's 30-day neighbour check (`qc` in `latest.json`), all countries, summed for the area shown; the *OpenAQ sensors* choice of the map item *Forecast accuracy (7 days)* (which compares with reference monitors, OpenAQ sensors or IQAir, one set of badges at a time; the (i) note and the area total follow the choice) with a badge per sensor (dashed rounded frame, grey bar, next-day share) and the total for the area shown under its name (next day and daily mean, with an (i) note), every horizon under the card's table on the admin page. They read high in humid air and the forecast at each sensor is corrected with its own readings, so this is closeness to the sensors, not to the true value.
- **Rain, the same way as PM2.5** (`scripts/radar.mjs`): the weather radar is the dense observation. Hourly, the last 2 h of RainViewer frames over Thailand (Thai Meteorological Department radars) are decoded (Universal Blue colours → dBZ → Marshall–Palmer rain rate) and averaged to 0.025° cells; the hourly means are scored against the ThaiWater gauges (adjustment factor gauge/radar over 7 days), replace ECMWF rain in the meteorology used by the smoke model where a radar sees, feed the ML rain experts as the truth (`rainobs-*.csv` column `radar_mmh`), and are published to the page (`rain-obs.json`, field `radar`). The latest frame is extrapolated along the echo motion (block cross-correlation) for 3 h. For 3–48 h the radar corrects ECMWF the way the monitors correct CAMS: every hour ECMWF is compared with the radar over the last 72 h for its timing (how many hours late its rain comes) and amount (a 0.25° radar/ECMWF factor field), and the page can blend ECMWF read later, ECMWF × the factor and the radar of the same hour on the latest observed day (diurnal persistence) by lead (`CFG.rainBlend`). The weights come only from `scripts/fit_rain_blend.py`, run on the station log `rainfc-*.csv` (every 3 h, all members by lead) against the radar truth in `rainobs-*.csv`: the first fit (15 Oct – 8 Nov 2026) kept the blend at leads of 1–2 h; from 3 h on the weights are zero (ECMWF alone caught more rain hours there). It is refitted monthly. The ML rain experts already take the radar as truth and as their recent-rain inputs. Outside radar coverage (Laos, Myanmar, Cambodia) nothing changes. **Radar coverage** is RainViewer's coverage mask AND the echo footprint (cells within 0.1° of a cell where the radar has shown an echo in at least 2 different hours, so single speckles of noise do not count; counts kept in `radar-cache.json`): the API mask alone marked 98 % of the box as seen (Nov 2026), so northern Laos, Cambodia and the open sea were stored as measured dry. On the map, in an observed hour (past or now) the radar or satellite values are drawn where they observe, and elsewhere the ECMWF forecast downscaled to 0.025° (0.25° open-data source) slightly faded (caption note); hours ahead show the forecast everywhere. Radar frames pass a noise filter first: an echo cell needs ≥ 11 echo cells in its 5 × 5 neighbourhood, which removes interference spokes and single speckles (they also no longer count toward the coverage). Outside the footprint the radar is missing and PARDIG / GSMaP / ECMWF are used; the footprint grows as rain is seen.
- **ECMWF rain at 0.25° over the whole map** (`scripts/ecmwf_pr.py`, ECMWF open data, CC BY 4.0): Open-Meteo's free limit gives the pipeline ECMWF at 0.25° over the north only (1° elsewhere), so the page's rain forecast looked coarse outside the north. Each run this step checks for a new 00/12 UTC run; it reads only the total precipitation of each step (byte ranges from the public Google Cloud / AWS mirrors, about 17 s), crops 92–110 E × 5–25 N, turns the accumulations into mean rates per 3-h (later 6-h) interval and writes `data/history/YYYY-MM/ecpr-<run>.json` (history branch, 35 days) and the page file `data/ecmwf-pr.json` (~0.14 MB; a run more than 36 h old is not published, so after a long failure of this step the page falls back to the Open-Meteo rain instead of showing an old forecast). The page uses it for the rain forecast everywhere (`metPrAt`), downscaled to 0.025° with the CHELSA pattern as before; the pipeline (smoke model, rain blend fits, weather ML) keeps the Open-Meteo rain, so the fitted weights stay valid.
- **Observed satellite rain** (`scripts/pardig.mjs`, `scripts/gsmap.mjs`): outside radar coverage the past hours use Google DeepMind PARDIG (0.1°, every 15 min, read anonymously from the public bucket `gs://pardig_1`, averaged to hourly means), with JAXA GSMaP Gauge_NOW/NRT for the newest hours PARDIG has not published yet (it runs ~4–5 h behind) and as the fallback. Against 675,601 ThaiWater gauge-hours (9–15 Nov 2026) PARDIG caught 71 % of rain hours ≥ 0.5 mm/h with 52 % false alarms, GSMaP 44 % with 68 %. Both are logged in `rainobs-*.csv` (`pardig_cell`, `pardig_fine`) and scored daily against the gauges over 14 days; the page goes back to GSMaP by itself if PARDIG stops catching at least as many rain hours with no larger share of false alarms (`obsRainCheck` in `scripts/update.mjs`). Limitation: the check counts only gauge rows that hold both values, so with GSMaP offline (below) it has no rows to decide on and PARDIG stays by default. PARDIG takes ECMWF fields among its inputs, so it is not used as the truth for fitting or judging ECMWF rain corrections (radar and gauges are); the one exception is the rain scores of the performance page (`scripts/perf.mjs`), which use GSMaP and fall back to PARDIG on days without GSMaP, naming those days in the note. GSMaP has been unavailable since 18 Nov 2026: JAXA took the distribution system offline after unauthorised access (JAXA press release of 24 Nov 2026), with no restoration date; the pinned sftp host key protects the pipeline from a replaced server (a changed key must be checked with JAXA before it is accepted).
- **PM2.5 correction v2, a candidate beside the live one** (`scripts/ml2.mjs`): in the first 47 days hours ≥ 37.5 µg/m³ were forecast far too low (measured 50, page 30 at 24 h; `analysis/highpm_replay.py`). Two causes: the live correction learns on ln(PM+1), which gives the median and pulls high values down, and it does not know a station's own habits (the peaks are short, local and recur at the same hours). v2 uses the same experts with the error in µg/m³ as the target and five station-history inputs known at issue time (same hour on the newest known day and the day before, 24-h maximum, 72-h 90th percentile, 7-day mean at that hour). It is computed and logged every hour at the hourly stations for leads ≤ 24 h (like v1, not at the 24-h-mean stations) (`correctedML2`; the live correction as `correctedML1`), and published only while a daily check over 14 days finds it at least as good as the live correction on all three counts: hours ≥ 37.5 caught, false-alarm share, RMSE.
- **Districts and sub-districts** (since 26 Nov 2026): `data/static/th-adm2.json` (928 amphoe, 1.2 MB, 290 kB gzipped) and `th-adm3.json` (7,425 tambon, 4.6 MB, 810 kB gzipped), built once by `scripts/admin_bounds.mjs` from the OCHA COD-AB Thailand boundaries of 2022 (Royal Thai Survey Department, `tha_admbnda_adm3_rtsd_20220121`; CC BY-IGO) as extracted in [prasertcbs/thailand_gis](https://github.com/prasertcbs/thailand_gis), simplified with mapshaper (districts dissolved from the simplified sub-districts, so their borders coincide), coordinates rounded to 4 decimals; the 10 Thai names the COD cuts at 16 characters are restored from `scripts/admin_names_fix.json`. The page loads them on first use: district borders from zoom 9 and names from zoom 10, sub-district borders from zoom 11 and names from zoom 12 (*Show on map* → *District and sub-district borders and names*); the *Warnings* tab lists the forecast *By district* (the province lattice of 0.1° points, or a district's centre where no point falls in it); a tapped place or *My location* is named by sub-district, district and province, with the district average at that hour. *Area shown* (Settings) is Thailand or the whole map; under Thailand a second menu picks one of the five Thai regions or one province; a province: the map is cut and fitted to it, its districts (borders and names) and sub-district borders are drawn at every zoom (sub-district names from zoom 10), and the *Warnings* list switches to its districts.
- **Thai province borders** (since 27 Nov 2026): the 77 Thai provinces in the page's `geoProv` block are the districts above dissolved (`scripts/province_bounds.mjs`, run by hand on both `index.html` and `index_modern.html`: the `geoProv` block sits in the pages' markup, which `scripts/build_modern.mjs` does not copy), so a chosen province's outline sits exactly on its districts' borders. Other countries' provinces stay World Bank Official Boundaries.
- **Station list for the logs** (since 27 Nov 2026): `data/history/stations.json` on the `history` branch names the station ids used in the per-run logs (name, Thai name, area as the provider gives it, position, country, provider, reference monitor or not, last date seen). `scripts/stationlist.mjs` merges the current stations into it once a day; stations that leave the list keep their entry.
- **Short-range nowcast at hourly stations** (since 28 Nov 2026, `scripts/pmnow.mjs`): the newest measured hour's error (log ratio to the forecast an earlier run made for that hour) is carried into the next 1–6 h, fading as e^(−lead/τ), and cut after 6 h; the forecast's shape is kept (Air4Thai hourly stations only). Tested before use (first version, τ = 2 h): Air4Thai hourly 16–27 Nov 2026 (high hours caught at 1 h 31 % → 52 %, false-alarm share unchanged, RMSE 6.93 → 6.58) and the hindcast smoke season 2026 (3 h RMSE −17 to −20 %, more caught, fewer false alarms; 9–12 h slightly worse, hence the cut). Logged as `correctedPre` / `correctedNC` in `fcst-*.csv`; it replaces the published station series only while its daily 14-day check passes (caught ≥, false-alarm share ≤, RMSE ≤; `state.pmNow.check`, per model). The map is not changed. The check rebuilds every row on the hours each run really had (half the Air4Thai stations publish an hour late). On those the first version caught more high hours but with more false alarms and a larger RMSE, so it failed. Version B (28 Nov 2026, chosen from 47 tested variants) uses only a measured hour at most 1 h old, clamps the error to ±0.5, fades with τ 1.5 h and skips the 20 stations whose values Air4Thai often revises: over the 14 days to 27 Nov at Air4Thai stations, hours ≥ 37.5 µg/m³ caught 1055 → 1181 of 4698, false alarms 50.8 → 49.3 %, RMSE 7.78 → 7.70; it also passes on 24 Oct – 13 Nov.
- **Where the air comes from**: tap a place on the map → *Where is the air from?* draws an ensemble of 27 backward trajectories (48 h or 24 h) arriving at 100, 500 and 1500 m above ground, HYSPLIT-style (9 members per height with the winds read ±0.25° around the parcel), from ECMWF winds at 10 m, 100 m, 925, 850 and 700 hPa (`data/met-traj.json`, loaded on first use), and lists the countries the air passed over and the fire hotspots near the paths.
- **Ventilation**: map layer and place-card readout of the ventilation index (ECMWF boundary-layer height × 100-m wind, m²/s, drawn at 0.025° with the wind adjusted for terrain exposure and sheltering — MicroMet weighting of Liston & Elder 2006, within ±40 %; below 2,000 poor, 2,000–6,000 moderate, above 6,000 good — the US NMC air-pollution-potential criterion of Stackpole 1967 / Gross 1970); the forecast summary gives each day's mean.
- **Automatic refresh**: an open page checks `data/stamp.json` every 3 minutes and loads a new run in place (keeping the chosen hour and place); a new page version reloads the page.

## How it runs

| Part | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| `scripts/silam.py`, `scripts/ecmwf_pr.py` | GitHub Actions, hourly (started by an external cron at :07; a schedule every 3 h at :37 is the backup) | a new SILAM run (NetCDF-4) and ECMWF open-data rain (GRIB2), when one is out; kept on the `history` branch |
| `scripts/update.mjs` | same job | stations (Air4Thai hourly, the BMA feed, OpenAQ), CAMS, ECMWF meteorology, radar / PARDIG / GSMaP / ThaiWater gauges, FIRMS → spike, neighbour-spike and stuck-sensor checks → fire-emission assimilation → Kalman filters → ML correction v1 (v2 candidate logged) → nowcast (logged; published while its check passes) → regression-kriging map and ML map field → `data/latest.json` and the logs |
| `scripts/verify.mjs` | same job | RMSE / bias / r by lead time → `data/verify.json` |
| `scripts/silam_chain.mjs` | same job | `update.mjs` again with SILAM in the place of CAMS → `data/silam/`, `data/history/silam/` |
| `scripts/perf.mjs` | same job | model performance page (`perf.html`, every 3 h from the logs) |
| `scripts/accuracy.mjs` | same job | accuracy per monitor for the public page (7-day hourly scores, 30-day daily-mean scores) → `data/accuracy.json` |
| deploy | same job | `_site` to GitHub Pages first, then the same files to Cloudflare Pages (https://sea-haf.pages.dev; skipped without its secrets, and its failure never stops the GitHub deploy) |
| `index.html` + `index_modern.html` | GitHub Pages (deployed by the workflow) | the site's default page (`/`) is the redesign (`index_modern.html` layout with the page code of `index.html`, built by `scripts/build_modern.mjs`); the original layout stays at `/classic.html`. Both read `data/*.json` and run the fire-smoke model in the browser |

Large, changing files (`latest.json`, `met.json`, `state.json`) are not committed: they are published to Pages and carried between runs in the Actions cache. The per-run logs (`data/history/YYYY-MM/`, last 35 days) live on the branch `history` and the ML training store (`data/mltrain/`, 2 years) on the branch `mltrain`; `main` keeps only the static files in `data/static/`.

No dependencies; Node 22 in the workflows. Python is used where files must be decoded or fitted: every hour `scripts/silam.py` (netCDF4) and `scripts/ecmwf_pr.py` (pygrib), each installing its package only when a new run is to be read; and, run by hand or monthly, `scripts/prclim.py` (rasterio, numpy; workflow `prclim.yml`) and `scripts/fit_rain_blend.py` (numpy).

## Setup

1. **Secrets** (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions; names only, the values never go into the repository):
   - `OPENAQ_API_KEY` (OpenAQ v3; required by `update.mjs`, also used by `climatology.yml`), `FIRMS_MAP_KEY` (NASA FIRMS hotspots);
   - `GSMAP_USER`, `GSMAP_PASS` (JAXA GSMaP sftp account), `IQAIR_API_KEY` (IQAir comparison layer; IQAir is not asked without it);
   - `DEV_USER`, `DEV_PASS` (username and password of the encrypted developer and performance pages, `scripts/lockpage.mjs`);
   - `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`, `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` (Cloudflare Pages deploy and `cloudflare-setup.yml`);
   - `CHAT_EXPORT_TOKEN` (the weekly chat training's read of `/api/export`, `chat-train.yml`; `cloudflare-setup.yml` stores it as the Pages secret `EXPORT_TOKEN`).
2. **Workflow permissions** (Settings → Actions → General): *Read and write permissions*.
3. **Pages** (Settings → Pages): Source **GitHub Actions**. The hourly workflow publishes `_site` as a Pages artifact: the redesign at `/`, the original layout at `/classic.html`, the encrypted `/dev.html` and `/perf.html`, and the data files.
4. Actions → *Hourly update* → *Run workflow* for the first run. The hourly runs are started by an external cron (`workflow_dispatch`); the workflow's own schedule (every 3 h) is only a backup.

Tunable settings are in the `CFG` block at the top of `scripts/update.mjs` (area, grid step, Kalman Q/R, regression-kriging settings `rk`, bias fade with lead time, persistence blend by lead); the rain and climatology blend weights (`rainBlend`, `climBlend`) are set only from their fit scripts.

## Data sources

Screening tool: the fire-smoke model has plume rise and wet removal but no chemistry (so no secondary aerosol) and no dry deposition, and smoke injected above the boundary layer is dropped rather than re-entrained; check against ground monitors before acting on a warning.
